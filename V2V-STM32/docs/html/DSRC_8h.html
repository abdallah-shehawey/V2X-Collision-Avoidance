Detailed Description

The V2V transport: the neighbor table and the over-the-air frame format shared with the ESP32 radio bridge.

Author
Abdallah Shehawey

DSRC is the layer every ADAS module sits on. It answers one question — which other vehicles are near me right now, and what are they doing — and keeps the answer in a table of at most MAX_NEIGHBORS entries, one per vehicle heard from recently.

The path a message takes

The STM32 has no radio of its own. An ESP32 bridges the air interface:

*   other car --ESP-NOW--> our ESP32 --UART1--> STM32   (receive)
*   STM32 --UART1--> our ESP32 --ESP-NOW--> other cars  (transmit)
*

On the way in, USART1's ISR only enqueues raw bytes; DSRC_RxCallback is then driven from vTask_ESP_Comm in task context, one byte at a time, and reassembles frames. On the way out, DSRC_SendNeighbor frames this vehicle's own Neighbor struct and hands it to the ESP32 every 100 ms.

Frame layout

*   +------------+---------------------------+----------+----------+
*   | START_BYTE |   Neighbor (21 bytes)     | checksum | END_BYTE |
*   |    0xAA    |        packed             |  1 byte  |   0x55   |
*   +------------+---------------------------+----------+----------+
*

A frame is only accepted if the start byte, the checksum and the end byte all agree, so a truncated or corrupted frame is dropped rather than acted on.

Warning
The Neighbor layout is a wire format, mirrored byte for byte in the ESP32 sketches (esp32/master, esp32/sniffer). Reordering, resizing or inserting a field on one side and not the other silently misaligns every field after it — the link keeps working and the data becomes nonsense. Change both sides together, always.
