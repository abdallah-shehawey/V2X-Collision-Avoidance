Direction classification thresholds

How far two vehicles' headings may differ and still count as travelling the same way, opposite ways, or across each other [degrees].

Note
These were widened from 30° to 40° deliberately. Each car reports the heading its own magnetometer measured, and two magnetometers rarely agree to better than a few tens of degrees. A tight threshold therefore kept classifying genuinely same-direction pairs as DIR_UNKNOWN, which silently switched every cooperative module off.

 Headings within this of each other → DIR_SAME.
#define HEADING_OPPOSITE_THRESHOLD
 Headings within this of 180° apart → DIR_OPPOSITE.
#define HEADING_CROSS_THRESHOLD
 Headings within this of 90° apart → DIR_CROSSING.
Shared safe-distance model

The one place the firmware decides how much room is "enough".

Both distance-based modules — local FCW and EEBL — use it, so a change here moves both, and the two can never drift out of agreement.

The gap scales with speed, because stopping distance does:

*   safe_cm     = max(Host_Speed[m/s] * SAFE_DIST_PER_MS, MIN_SAFE_DISTANCE)
*   critical_cm = safe_cm * CRITICAL_RATIO
*
Note
The constants are sized for the prototype, not a real car: a small vehicle whose top speed is around 0.5 m/s, operating in corridors where obstacles sit tens of centimetres away. At that scale the speed term is small and MIN_SAFE_DISTANCE is what actually governs — it is the dominant floor, not a rarely-hit corner case.
#define SAFE_DIST_PER_MS
 Centimetres of safe gap per 1 m/s of speed.
#define MIN_SAFE_DISTANCE
 Floor on the safe gap [cm], whatever the speed.
#define CRITICAL_RATIO
 Fraction of the safe gap below which the risk is critical.

Variables

Host vehicle state for the current cycle

SafetyEngine_voidUpdate latches these from G_stHostVehicleState once at the top of each 50 ms cycle, and the modules then read them as they walk the neighbor table.

Latching once is what guarantees every module in a given cycle reasons about the same vehicle state, even though the sensors keep updating.
